Changes these 2024 rules:Combat

Choose the response before rolling the attack and defense dice.

Parry

Roll 1d20 + proficiency bonus + Strength or Dexterity modifier, plus applicable Momentum.

If the Parry wins, the attack deals no normal weapon damage and the defender gains Momentum. On a natural 20, inflict one Critical Injury on the attacker even if the Parry loses. Use the defender's weapon damage type for that injury; an unarmed Parry normally uses Bludgeoning.

Dodge

Roll 1d20 + proficiency bonus + Dexterity modifier, plus applicable Momentum.

If the Dodge wins, the attack deals no normal weapon damage and the defender gains Momentum. On a natural 20, negate all damage from the attack even if the Dodge loses. If the attacker wins the opposed totals despite that natural 20, the attacker still gains Momentum.

Both dice show 20. By default, Dodge takes precedence: the attack deals no damage and causes no Critical Injury to the defender. The GM may choose either of these alternate rules for the campaign:

  • Dodge prevents normal damage, but the attacker's natural 20 still causes one Critical Injury.
  • Cancel both natural-20 effects and resolve the exchange from its totals.

Tank the Hit

Make no defense roll. Add the defender's Strength modifier to Damage Negation for this hit. A negative Strength modifier reduces Negation.

Tanking cannot win an opposed exchange, change Momentum, or cause a defensive critical.

Unopposed

Make no defense roll. The attack hits unless its attack die is a natural 1.

Surprised

Make no defense roll. A successful attack that deals damage causes one Critical Injury in addition to normal damage. Surprise adds no bonus to the injury roll. Do not double the damage dice.

If the same attack also causes an injury through a natural 20 or Excess Damage, resolve one injury, applying Excess Damage when present. Modifiers from other injury rules still apply.

This response is an addition to the 2024 Surprise rule, which normally imposes Disadvantage on Initiative. The 2014 Assassin's automatic-critical feature does not cause a second injury; that legacy subclass requires a separate conversion.
