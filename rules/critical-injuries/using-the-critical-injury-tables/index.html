When a creature suffers a Critical Injury:

  1. Choose one damage type using the rules below.
  2. Roll 1d100 + Excess Damage + 10 per existing Critical Injury.
  3. Find the modified total on the matching damage-type table. Totals can exceed 100.
  4. Apply the result's effect and record its Bleeding, treatment, recovery, and magical-healing requirements. Result-specific instructions take precedence over the general procedure.

Choose One Damage Type

Use the only damage type dealt by the triggering attack or effect. If it dealt more than one type, use the type that contributed the most damage before Damage Negation. On a tie, the attacker chooses. For a Parry critical, use the defender's weapon damage type; an unarmed Parry normally uses Bludgeoning. A creature immune to the chosen type cannot suffer a Critical Injury of that type.

No Separate Hit-Location Roll

Do not roll hit location. When a result names a plausible limb, organ, or sense, use the circumstances of the attack to determine which one. If several outcomes are equally plausible, the injured creature chooses.

Bleeding

Bleeding X is cumulative and is an explicit exception to the normal rule that conditions do not stack with themselves. At the start of each of a bleeding creature's turns, roll one of its Hit Dice and lose that many Hit Points, plus 1 for each Bleeding level beyond the first. Roll one die, not the creature's Hit Dice pool; the die explodes recursively. For example, Bleeding 3 with a d10 Hit Die causes 1d10 + 2 Hit Point loss.

After that loss, a creature at 0 Hit Points becomes Unconscious. If it is bleeding and Unconscious, make one roll of 1d20 - (Bleeding level - 1). On a modified result of 1 or lower, it bleeds out and dies unless Fate or an appropriate effect intervenes.

Bleeding bypasses Damage Negation and does not cause Excess Damage or another Critical Injury. Each Bleeding level that ends adds 1 Exhaustion level to any existing Exhaustion. At Exhaustion 6, the creature dies as normal. When a creature has multiple Hit Die types, use its largest for now.

Treatment and Recovery

An injury's treatment check requires appropriate supplies and 10 minutes of work unless its effect sets a more urgent deadline. When an effect gives a deadline measured in turns, attempting that treatment is an action. Successful treatment removes Bleeding caused by that injury and begins its natural recovery. Complete the short or long rests specified in that result to end the injury and its temporary effects. Failed treatment can be attempted again after a short or long rest.

Magic cast with at least the listed spell-slot level can repair the injury if the spell is appropriate to the harm. Result-specific recovery text takes precedence; ordinary healing does not regrow missing anatomy, replace an erased mind, or restore life. A general First Aid action and Bleeding's interaction with ordinary magical Hit Point healing remain open. Treatment DCs, recovery times, and magical thresholds are first-playtest values.
