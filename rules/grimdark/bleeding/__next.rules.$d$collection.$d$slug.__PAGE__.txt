3:T68e,<p>Bleeding has a cumulative level. Add new Bleeding levels to existing levels; this condition is an exception to the normal rule that a condition does not stack with itself.</p>
<p>At the start of a bleeding creature's turn:</p>
<ol>
<li>Roll one die of its Hit Die type, recursively exploding maximum results. Do not roll or expend its Hit Dice pool. If it has more than one Hit Die type, use the largest in this draft.</li>
<li>Lose Hit Points equal to that roll plus <code>Bleeding level − 1</code>.</li>
<li>If the creature is now at 0 Hit Points, it becomes Unconscious.</li>
<li>If it is bleeding and Unconscious, make one bleed-out roll: <code>1d20 − (Bleeding level − 1)</code>. On a modified result of 1 or lower, it dies unless Fate or another effect prevents death.</li>
</ol>
<p>Bleeding bypasses Negation. Its Hit Point loss does not cause Excess Damage or another Critical Injury.</p>
<p><strong>Example.</strong> Bleeding 3 on a creature with a d10 Hit Die causes one exploding d10 plus 2 Hit Point loss. If that leaves the creature at 0 Hit Points, it becomes Unconscious and rolls <code>1d20 − 2</code> to check for death.</p>
<p><strong>Ending Bleeding.</strong> Each removed Bleeding level adds 1 Exhaustion level. Removing Bleeding 3 adds 3 Exhaustion; it does not replace any Exhaustion the creature already has. Reaching Exhaustion 6 is fatal under the 2024 rule.</p>
<p>Successful treatment of a Critical Injury removes the Bleeding caused by that injury. A general First Aid action and the effect of ordinary magical Hit Point healing on Bleeding are unresolved. The use of the largest Hit Die for a multiclass creature remains provisional.</p>
